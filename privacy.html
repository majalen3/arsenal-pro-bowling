Privacy

Draft, not legal advice. Have an attorney review before launch. This page was written from what the app and its server actually do on [EFFECTIVE DATE]. The yellow parts are blanks the owner must fill in.

The short version

  • Your balls, games, bag and notes are saved on your phone first. The app works fully offline.
  • Nothing is sent to our server unless you turn on an account, an optional backup code, My Team, or look up a ball's specs.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party scripts, and the app sets no cookies.
  • You can export or delete an account and its server copy in the app. You can delete everything on your phone by clearing the site's data.

1. What stays on your phone

Arsenal Pro is a web app. It keeps this in your browser's storage on your device:

  • Your arsenal and games: balls you add, layouts, bags, nights, games, shots, centers, notes, and any LaneTrax or file imports you choose to make (stored as the events the app builds from them). This lives in IndexedDB.
  • Your profile and settings (localStorage): hand, style, ball speed, rev rate and the optional PAP and axis tilt you type in, look and text size, flags such as "onboarded", and the teammates you block (the server is never told who you block).
  • A private device code (localStorage). It is only used if you turn on backup-code sync (section 3).
  • Account sign-in and team cache (localStorage), only if you sign in: a sign-in token (not your password) and the last team view so My Team opens offline.
  • On-device counters (localStorage, "Tests and usage" in Me): how often screens were opened. These are never sent anywhere.
  • Short-lived tab state (sessionStorage): where you were and whether a game is open.
  • Offline files (service worker cache): the app's own code and pictures. If you use the drill-card reader, its reading tools (about 5 MB) are saved too.

The app sets no cookies. Photos of a drill card are read on your phone and are never uploaded; only text you confirm is saved.

2. Lookups: what leaves your phone when you add a ball

When you search for a ball's specs, the app sends the brand and ball name (or a maker link you paste) to our server. The server reads public maker pages and public references and sends back the specs and where each came from. Your account or device code is not attached to this request. The server keeps a shared cache of those public pages, with nothing about you in it.

4. Who else is involved

  • GitHub Pages hosts the app's files. GitHub receives your IP address and browser details when you load the app, under its own privacy statement.
  • Render hosts our server and database (US, Oregon region). It handles the requests described above and keeps its own standard server logs, under its own privacy policy.
  • Maker websites and public references are read by our server, not by your phone, when you look up a ball.
  • The app has no analytics, advertising, social, payment or font services. The share button uses your phone's own share sheet; what you share goes where you send it.
  • Our server's own request log records the method, path, status and time of each request. It does not log request contents. A short-lived in-memory counter uses your IP address (and, for sync, your device or account) to limit abuse; it is not saved to the database. The server log also holds the standard connection details Render keeps.

5. Keeping, exporting and deleting your data

  • On your phone: "Download backup" in Me saves everything as a file. To delete it all, clear this site's data in your browser settings (or remove the installed app). We cannot do that for you.
  • Account: in Me, "Export my data" downloads your account copy and team footprint; "Delete account and server data" deletes your account, every sign-in and the server copy. Your phone keeps its own data.
  • Teams: leaving a team removes what you shared and your notes. Deleting your account does the same on every team. If the secretary leaves, the longest-standing member takes over.

8. Security

Traffic to our server uses HTTPS. Passwords and recovery keys are stored only as one-way hashes, and session tokens only as keyed hashes. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise it. If you lose your password and recovery key we cannot restore the account.
